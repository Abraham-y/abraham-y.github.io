Midterms 2026: model vs. markets

A polls-and-fundamentals model of every Senate and governor race, compared against Kalshi and Polymarket prices, plus a county-level projection that updates as votes are counted. Probabilities are for the Democrat, or the independent where one is the main challenger. Model output only, not betting advice.

Before the count: where the model and the market disagree

Races the market prices between 4% and 96%, sorted by the size of the gap. "Holds" means the gap kept its sign and stayed above 3 points under all 11 alternative model settings.

Method

  • Poll average weighted by recency, sample size and likely-voter screens, with partisan sponsors down-weighted and shifted 2 points; blended with published averages and a fundamentals prior (2024 presidential margin, a D+8.7 generic-ballot environment, and each candidate's past over- or under-performance).
  • Error sizes come from 646 Senate and governor races in 538's archived polls (1998–2022): about 5.6 points on the margin, 3 of it shared nationally, with fatter-than-normal tails.
  • Blended with forecaster probabilities and expert ratings, weighted by source quality. Chamber control uses a correlated simulation across all races.
  • On election night, county results are compared with each county's 2024 baseline and expected turnout, corrected for which ballot types each state counts first, and the uncounted vote is simulated.
  • Not yet backtested against past market prices, so the gaps are disagreements with the market, not a proven edge.
