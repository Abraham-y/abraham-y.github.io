I work on reinforcement learning and post-training, with a focus on what those methods do to a model's safety properties: whether its chain of thought stays honest, whether internal signals stay trustworthy once you train on them, and which design choices quietly trade oversight for capability. What I optimize for

  • Models that stay overseeable as they get stronger. Chain-of-thought monitoring only works if the reasoning we read is the reasoning that drove the answer. I want to know which training choices preserve that, and which erode it while the text still looks fine.
  • Post-training that doesn't trade safety for capability by accident. Reward design, distillation, and RL loops each have side effects on honesty and controllability. Those effects should be measured, not assumed.
  • Interpretability artifacts wired into training carefully. A probe that is safe to read can be dangerous to optimize against. The access a training loop gives an internal signal should be a deliberate decision.
  • Measurements that survive scrutiny. Pre-registered designs, placebo controls, bootstrap intervals, and numbers that regenerate from committed artifacts. A safety metric with a hidden confound is worse than no metric.

Research

Two preprints, two workshop papers under review, and a proposal I'm working towards.

Math Distillation Decouples Chain-of-Thought from Behavior on a Reasoning Model

Abraham Yeung · preprint, June 2026 · PDF

After math chain-of-thought fine-tuning, a planted cue moves the model's answers exactly as much as before, but the CoT stops mentioning it, with acknowledgment falling from 25% to 3% while accuracy improves. The behavior stays, the confession disappears, and a capability-only check would pass the model. Pre-registered, with bootstrap confidence intervals.

Reading vs. Writing a Near-Oracle Internal Verifier: How RL Design Determines Whether a Correctness Probe Is Safe

Abraham Yeung, Anagha Ramaswamy · preprint, June 2026 · PDFCode

Outcome RL grows a near-perfect linear correctness probe inside a small model. Used read-only, as a best-of-n selector, it gives large gains. Used as the reward, it Goodharts badly. The less policy-gradient access the probe gets, the safer it is, which bounds how interpretability signals should be wired into RL.

Alembic: Auditing Cue-Injection Faithfulness Metrics

2026 · workshop paper under review

The most-cited chain-of-thought faithfulness measurement inherits its headline conclusion, that smaller models are less faithful, from size-dependent error terms. A content-free placebo insertion reproduces up to 85% of the measured cue influence at the smallest scale. Replicated in an independent run, with up to half the effect traced to an unset serving default and to task-family sampling.

Activation Cache Compression for Sparse Autoencoder Training

2026 · workshop paper under review

How aggressively can cached LLM activations be compressed before sparse autoencoders retrained on them diverge, and how do you measure that validly? About 170 GPU-hours of pre-registered experiments on a 97-test library of codecs, SAE trainers, and streaming fidelity metrics. Every published number regenerates from committed artifacts.

research note

Do confidence rewards quietly hurt CoT monitorability? Self-certainty and entropy-minimizing rewards pay a model for sounding sure. Oversight needs it to say its doubts out loud. A proposed experiment on OLMo-3 to measure whether these rewards reduce monitor recall at fixed CoT length.
