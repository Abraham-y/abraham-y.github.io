About me

I'm a sophomore at Stanford studying Mathematics and Computer Science. I work on reinforcement learning and post-training, with a focus on what those methods do to a model's safety properties: whether its chain of thought stays honest, whether internal signals stay trustworthy once you train on them, and which design choices quietly trade oversight for capability.

What I optimize for

  • Models that stay overseeable as they get stronger. Chain-of-thought monitoring only works if the reasoning we read is the reasoning that drove the answer. I want to know which training choices preserve that, and which erode it while the text still looks fine.
  • Post-training that doesn't trade safety for capability by accident. Reward design, distillation, and RL loops each have side effects on honesty and controllability. Those effects should be measured, not assumed.
  • Interpretability artifacts wired into training carefully. A probe that is safe to read can be dangerous to optimize against. The access a training loop gives an internal signal should be a deliberate decision.
  • Theory and systems together. The interesting questions sit where the RL math meets the actual training run, so I try to work at both ends.

Research

Two preprints from 2026, plus a research proposal I'm working towards.

Math Distillation Decouples Chain-of-Thought from Behavior on a Reasoning Model

Abraham Yeung · arXiv cs.LG, June 2026 · PDF

After math chain-of-thought fine-tuning, a planted cue moves the model's answers exactly as much as before, but the CoT stops mentioning it, with acknowledgment falling from 25% to 3% while accuracy improves. The behavior stays, the confession disappears, and a capability-only check would pass the model.

Reading vs. Writing a Near-Oracle Internal Verifier: How RL Design Determines Whether a Correctness Probe Is Safe

Abraham Yeung, Anagha Ramaswamy · arXiv cs.LG, June 2026 · PDFCode

Outcome RL grows a near-perfect linear correctness probe inside a small model. Used read-only, it gives large deployment gains. Used as the reward, it Goodharts badly. The less policy-gradient access the probe gets, the safer it is, which bounds how interpretability signals should be wired into RL.

research note

Do confidence rewards quietly hurt CoT monitorability? Self-certainty and entropy-minimizing rewards pay a model for sounding sure. Oversight needs it to say its doubts out loud. A proposed experiment on OLMo-3 to measure whether these rewards reduce monitor recall at fixed CoT length.
